# Changelog

## 28 November 2026

### Two mandirs, rounder wheels, and something different on every shelf
- Two Hindu temples open in Port Concord. Shri Samudra Mandir rises over Signet Row, with a tall carved shikhara,
  a saffron flag and a pillared porch; Sagar Tat Mandir is a small whitewashed shrine on a Shallows islet, facing the
  sea. Leave your shoes at the steps, ring the brass bell as you go in, and light a diya at the shrine to be back to
  full health.
- Car, van, taxi and bus wheels are now real wheels: round tyres with tread and alloy or steel rims, set flat against
  the side of the body and turning with the car whichever way it faces.
- Every bookshelf in the city holds its own handful of books. Homes keep stories, poems, a harbour cookbook and a
  mystery; City Hall keeps the city charter, its history and the council minutes; offices keep tide tables, a
  bookkeeping handbook and merchants' letters. The six primers on keeping money safe turn up everywhere.
- Notice boards pin up their own notices: council meetings, lost cats and festival nights at City Hall, job ads and
  the kettle rota in offices, signing rotas and forged-note warnings at the Mint Hall. Today's quests are still the
  first card on every board.
- Indoors, the room always fills the screen: scrolling can zoom in for a closer look but never out into the dark
  around the room, and zooming out on the street no longer shrinks the rooms you walk into.

### Every building has a name, and more of them open
- All 95 buildings in Port Concord now have their own name: shops after their keepers ("Marlow & Daughters,
  Tailors"), homes by street address ("14 Kelp Row"), offices after the firms inside. No two share a name.
- A name board hangs over each door as you walk up, the door button says where you are going, and inside, the top
  of the screen names the building and its district.
- Six more kinds of building open their doors, each furnished its own way:
  - **City Hall**: today's quests on the notice board and the city's records on the shelf.
  - **Harbour Chapel**: a quiet place to light a candle and rest, which puts you back to full health.
  - **Pixel Pier Arcade**: step up to a cabinet to play the Academy drills.
  - **Saltmarket Fish Hall**: fishcakes, grilled fish and harbour chowder at the counter, paid with your notes.
  - **Offices**: a notice board, a bookshelf, and desks for the firms of Port Concord.
  - **Tidewater Temple**: a new landmark in a garden in Low Tide. Ring its great bronze bell, or sit on a cushion
    and breathe until you are back to full health.
- A spot right inside a door no longer gets in the way of leaving: pressing E by the door always walks you out.

### Try it on, see it on you
- Shops that sell things to wear now show your guardian at the top. Tap "Try on" next to anything to see it on
  you straight away, in the shop and out in the room, before you pay. Whatever you buy is put on at once.
- Haircuts, hair colours, the beard trim and surfboards now show on your guardian. The wardrobe has a place for
  each of them, and a change there shows on you the moment you pick it.
- A robe bought from the tailor or the surf shop now colours your whole robe in Port Concord and Beach Town. In
  matches only its trim changes, so everyone keeps the seat colour that tells players apart.
- Changing clothes twice in quick succession no longer leaves other players seeing the first look.

### Books to read and something to cook
- Every home has a bookcase. Pick a book from the shelf and read it page by page: six short books about how a
  federation stays honest, from the Byzantine generals to why ecash notes come in powers of two.
- Cook in your kitchen: make tea, toast or noodle soup, and you are back to full health.

### Rooms fill the screen
- Inside a building, the room now covers the whole screen on any size of display, and the camera follows you
  round it. Zooming out no longer shrinks the room into a box with empty space around it.

### Things to do indoors
- Every place you can use inside a building now has a gold tile on the floor. It lights up when you are close
  enough, and a button tells you what it does: press E or tap it.
- At home: rest in bed to get back to full health, change clothes at the wardrobe, or catch up on the Chronicle on
  the TV.
- In the Mint Hall: vault your pocket at the counter, just as at the hall's walls outside, and read today's quests
  on the notice board.
- At the hospital: step up to the desk to be patched up.
- Shops point you to their counter as you walk in.

### Every building its own room, and shops you can buy from
- No two shops or houses look alike inside any more: every building is furnished its own way, in its own size and
  finishes. Each shop is a tailor, barber, hat shop, surf shop or café, fitted out for its trade with clothes rails
  and mirrors, barber chairs, hat stands, surfboard racks or café tables, and its counter dressed to match.
- Walk up to a shop's counter and press E (or tap "Shop at …") to browse its wares and pay with the notes in your
  wallet, just as at the Beach Town stalls.
- Every house is your own private home: a kitchen, a wardrobe, a TV with a sofa, a table and a bed, different in
  every house you walk into.
- Inside, the room now fills the screen and sits in the cut-away corner of its building, instead of floating in an
  empty purple space.

### Nobody walks through anybody
- Guardians no longer pass through each other: bump into someone and you both ease apart, on the street and
  indoors. Nobody can be pinned in a doorway, because a push always gives way.
- A car you drive stops against other cars instead of sliding through them, and the city's traffic brakes and waits
  behind your car.
- Waving, cheering, dancing and the shaka now raise real arms: robe sleeves that come out at the shoulders, bend at
  the elbow, catch the light like the robe and end in a bell cuff. The hands are the guardian's own, dark and
  faceted like the face under the hood and edged in the same warm light, sized in proportion with the body.

### A ferry between Port Concord and Beach Town
- A ferry is moored at the Port Concord seafront, under a blue FERRY sign and marked on the minimap, and another at
  the end of the Beach Town pier. Stand by it and press E (or tap "Ferry to …") to sail across.
- The crossing takes about eight seconds: you stand on the foredeck while the harbour falls away behind, gulls wheel
  overhead and the other island comes up ahead. Tap or press E after the first second to skip the rest; with reduced
  motion on, it is a short card instead.
- You step off at the other island's pier, and the ferry back leaves from the same spot.
- Beach Town still opens at Rank 3 or with four Academy stars. Until then the button says it is locked and tells you
  what opens it.
- The server now checks that lock on every way into Beach Town, by ferry or from the island map, so it can't be
  skipped by joining directly.

### Step inside: shops, the Mint Hall, the hospital and your own flat
- Walk up to a shop, the Mint Hall, the hospital or any house and press E (or tap the door button) to go in.
  Leave by pressing E at the inner door or by simply walking out through the doorway.
- Every house door leads to your own private home that nobody else can enter. Shops, the Mint Hall and the
  hospital are shared: you see the other guardians inside, up to twelve at once, and nobody out on the street.
- The hospital patches you up as you come in. Nobody can punch indoors, and you can't duck inside within ten
  seconds of being hit, from a car, or while knocked down.
- Each room is furnished: shelves and a till in the shops, columns, banners and a round vault door in the Mint
  Hall, beds, drips and a front desk in the hospital, and a bed, sofa, books and pictures in your flat, lit by
  sunlight from the windows.

### A more painterly Port Concord: water, streets and trees
- The sea now shades smoothly from turquoise at the quays to deep blue in open water, with ripples, glints and
  foam where it laps the shore. Sloping shores are cut along diagonals instead of tile steps, and boardwalks end in
  a dark edge beam that throws shade on the water.
- Roads have worn tyre lanes, white edge lines, drains, manholes and the odd crack; pavements have slab joints and
  raised kerbs; lawns are mown in stripes.
- Buildings cast soft shadows east across the ground, with a dark seam where their walls meet it.
- Street trees come in three kinds, a round broadleaf, a tall poplar and a pine, shaded from the west with leaf
  flecks and a shadow on the ground.

### Ships and cars of every kind
- Ships have real hulls now: a curved bow, a squared stern, a banded waterline, a lit deck with railings, a shadow
  and reflection on the water, a foaming wake and a bow wave. Ferries have one or two passenger decks, lifeboats and
  a striped funnel; cargo ships carry stacked, ribbed containers under yellow cranes; sailboats fly one or two
  curved sails; fishing boats are trawlers with outriggers and nets or crabbers stacked with pots. Every ship has
  its own colours and layout.
- Cars come in seven body styles (sedan, hatchback, SUV, van, pickup, taxi and bus) and sixteen paints, with tinted
  glass, rims, lamps and a shadow; traffic keeps its gaps by each car's own length.

### Emotes you act out
- The six emote buttons fold into one smiley button that opens a picker, each emote named on hover and still on
  keys 1 to 6.
- Emotes are now things your guardian does instead of an icon over their head: a hand that waves, a jump for joy
  with both hands up and confetti, a little dance with music notes, hearts floating up, a laugh that shakes them,
  and a shaka. Everyone nearby sees it, in the city and on the beach.

## 27 November 2026

### Story scenes you can read, with faces
- Story scenes now play one line at a time: each line types at a readable pace, breathing at commas and full stops,
  and waits for Space or a tap before the next. Text speed (slow, normal, fast, instant), an Auto mode that holds each
  line long enough to read, and a History panel of everything said so far sit under the dialogue.
- The speaker's portrait sits beside each line, drawn from the same character art as the city, and wears the face the
  line is said with: calm, happy, angry, worried, surprised, sad, smug, whispering or curious. It blinks, moves its
  mouth while the line types, and the character not speaking waits dimmed across from them. Phone calls and the old
  voicemail get the device they come out of.
- A timed choice's clock now starts once the whole crew has read up to it, not when the scene begins, and a crew
  waits for its slowest reader (up to a limit, so nobody can stall it). Lines the crew moved past go into History.

### Lines said the way they are meant
- Each face has its own delivery: an angry line shakes and is said faster, a surprised one pops and its speaker
  jumps, a sad one is said slowly as its speaker sinks, a whisper is quiet and on the breath.
- Every character has a talking voice, a short beep per few letters at their own pitch, lower when angry, higher when
  surprised, rising at the end of a question; the phone call sounds like a phone and the voicemail like an old tape.
  A Beeps button in the story panel turns them off.
- In the city, three talking dots float over whoever is speaking, and emotes stay up for four seconds, following the
  guardian who sent them.

### A cleaner story screen
- The story panel keeps just Auto and History, as quiet text buttons; text speed and talk beeps moved to Settings.
- "Space or tap to continue" shows on your first few lines only; the arrow says it after that.
- While a scene plays, the map, crew code and clue list step aside so the scene has the screen.

## 26 November 2026

### Spoken noir vocals
- Spoken noir intro vocals now introduce each scene mood once on entry (and stay silent while story dialogue is open),
  with a separate Vocals on/off toggle in settings (saved in `bc.audio`, default on; credited in `CREDITS.md`).

### French, Swahili, Hausa, isiZulu and Hindi
- The game and whole story (all 1,855 lines across every act, side case, end card, note and name tag) are now in
  French, Swahili, Hausa, isiZulu and Hindi as well as English and Spanish. Hausa, isiZulu and Hindi are marked beta
  until a native speaker reviews them, and each story catalog loads only when its language is picked.

### The story in Spanish, and lamplit nights
- The whole story plays in Spanish when Spanish is picked: all four acts of The Cold Key, season two and three, every
  side case, the casebook, the end cards, the notes and the name tags. That is 1,855 lines, written for Latin
  America. The Spanish lines load only when Spanish is picked, so English players download nothing extra; any line
  missing a translation shows in English, and a test fails the build if one is missing.
- The shade over ground your guardian can't see is gone. People out of sight still don't show; the city just isn't
  cut into dark wedges any more.
- Story nights are lit by the city: every street lamp throws a warm pool on the road and glows at its head, and a
  soft light around your guardian keeps the way ahead readable. Buildings stand in front of the light, not under it.
- The arrow pointing to your next stop is a crisp gold chevron that follows your guardian smoothly, turns and bobs
  gently, and fades in and out instead of popping.

### Controls that follow the streets, and a smoother city
- The arrow keys and WASD now follow the streets: one key walks straight down a road instead of up the screen and
  into a wall. The touch stick still points on screen.
- Cars drive like cars on the keyboard: W or ↑ for gas, S or ↓ to brake and reverse, A/D or ←/→ to steer. On the touch
  stick you still point where you want to go. Parked cars now face along their street toward open road, instead of
  all pointing the same way (some nose-first at the harbour wall).
- Everyone faces the way they walk, like a real person: walking up the screen you see their back, walking down you see
  their face. Guardians, story characters, townsfolk and the island explorer all turn; from behind, glasses, ties,
  pearls and Lumley's camera are hidden while hats, scarves and carried bags still show.
- Cars are solid: you bump into a parked or driven car instead of walking through it, and a driven car stops for
  anyone touching any part of its body. Nobody gets trapped: if you are already against a car, you can always walk
  away from it.
- Space jumps in the city and Beach Town: a real arc under gravity, the shadow shrinking beneath you and a puff of
  dust on landing. Everyone who can see you sees the jump; the server paces it so nobody can spam it.
- Every car is redrawn as a modern car: a rounded body, a sloped tinted glass cabin, alloy wheels, mirrors, a sheen
  on the roof and LED strips, white in front and red behind.
- Street lights are slim modern LED lamps with a small warm glow, instead of a big yellow blob that washed over
  anyone walking past; your own guardian is marked by a faint gold glow instead of a thick gold ring.
- Street lamps are thin posts now, not a tile of wall: you brush past them. Walking straight into a corner or post you
  nearly clear slips you round it instead of stopping dead; flat walls still stop you.
- Path searches, sight checks and torch cones now cost a third to a quarter of what they did (same results, checked
  against the old code on 10,200 random queries). Off-camera buildings, trees and lamps are no longer drawn every
  frame, and the city stops building throwaway arrays each frame.
- Mags no longer searches the whole city twenty times a second when nobody can reach her.

### Characters you can tell apart, and playtest feedback
- Every story character now dresses as who they are: Mr Fennick's bowler and satchel, Lumley's beret and camera,
  Perpetua Glass's bun, white shades and pearls, Mr Varnish's top hat, monocle and briefcase, Marco's chef's hat, Nell's
  sou'wester, Rex's megaphone and more. Guardians keep the plain hood, which is now their mark. Shoppers in The Clock
  and the townsfolk in the city, the plaza and Beach Town wear everyday outfits, so no two in a row look alike.
- A Feedback tile on the menu, and "How was it?" on story and match end cards, send a 1–5 rating and a short note.
  Nothing about the player is sent or stored. `docs/playtest.md` explains how to run a session with friends and read
  the notes.

### Rule tiers and Redis scaling
- Council matches now come in rule tiers: Apprentice (tasks and council votes), Journeyman (adds signing ceremonies
  and forgeries) and the Full council (adds network cuts and disconnects). Quick matches follow the least experienced
  guardian in the room, and the host of a private lobby picks. The server refuses anything outside the match's tier,
  and the tier picker and its notes are in English and Spanish.
- Several server processes can now share one Redis: matchmaking, room codes and per-network limits work across
  processes, Redis keys are namespaced and expire, and a Redis outage degrades instead of crash-looping.

### Spanish
- The game now speaks Spanish as well as English. The menu, lobby, settings, HUD, toasts, error messages, end and
  reward cards, progress pages and the casebook follow your browser's language, and a new Language picker in Settings
  lets you switch at any time and remembers your pick. Story dialogue and act text stay in English for now.

### Discord Activity and web portals
- The same game client now runs as a Discord Activity and inside web game portals. In Discord, players sign in with
  the `identify` scope only and their progress is saved to a guest profile; other players still see only the nickname
  they choose. Nothing stores a Discord id, token, name or avatar.
- Portal builds (`--mode portal`) skip the offline service worker and report loading and gameplay to CrazyGames or
  Poki when the portal provides them. Framing stays off unless `FRAME_ANCESTORS` allows it.

### The casebook: six side cases
- A new Casebook tile on the menu opens six one-night side cases, "ripped from the ledger": each inspired by a real
  event and retold with invented people. Play them in any order, alone or with a crew of up to four. The casebook
  marks the ones you've solved, and each pays 150 to 200 play sats the first time.
- **The Popcorn Tin.** A friendly race with Brannigan round a burglar's yard in Low Tide. He searches the obvious
  places first; the tin is in the kennel, and Biscuit the dog won't get off it unless someone rattles the bins.
- **Borrowed Number.** Pip's phone number was moved to a stranger's SIM, and his savings went with the text codes.
  Find his birthday, address and first pet out in the open, then walk into the phone shop, where the clerk isn't one.
- **Follow the Coins.** Read a public chain with Brannigan: the odd amount is the change, coins spent together share
  an owner. Find the reused address on a market tip jar (out of the music video's ring light) and the rapper who
  boasted about it.
- **The Dead Man's Vault.** An exchange boss lost at sea left his vault key in five shares on five graveyard benches;
  any three open it, which is lucky, because two plaques were stolen. Dodge Mrs Bellweather's lantern. The vault is
  empty. So was the coffin.
- **The Forgotten Password.** Kit's late brother left him a drive with three guesses left. Visit the three places
  they shared; each plays a memory of Sol that gives away a piece of his password. Then type. Both endings close
  the case.
- **Pizza Night.** Gather toppings round Saltmarket (no anchovies, ever), then run two hot pizzas across town past
  the gulls to Benny, who pays ten thousand coins for them. Ten years later, that's a lot of coins.
- Toasts now show above the story bar on phones, near the bottom of the screen, instead of hiding behind it.

### Drive anywhere in Port Concord
- Cars are parked kerbside in every district and by the arrival point. Walk up and press E (or "Get in"); point
  where you want to go with the keys or the touch stick and the car steers there, and points behind reverse it.
  E again gets you out beside the car. Abandoned cars are towed back to their spot after four minutes.
- The server drives every car: it stops dead against walls, water, people on foot and other cars, and lets go of the
  pedal if a driver's input stops arriving. Nobody can punch, or be punched, from a car, and toughs leave drivers be.
