# Changelog

## 25 November 2026

### Season two, act four: The Last Alert
- The end of season two. Ozzy traces the voice on the Mint channel to the gatehouse at Gullmoor tip, and Ivy
  explains: the Mint's first radio had an alert key, and anything signed with it every guardian's radio obeyed.
- It's Mags. She still has the key, Rex's volunteers are combing the tip for it, and there's one way to make it
  useless: read it out on the old aerial, to everyone. Walk her there. She follows whoever is nearest, slower than
  you, and if a volunteer's torch finds her she hurries back to the hatch.
- At the aerial, tell her what to do with it, or leave it to her. Then dawn, and the crossword. Remembers whether you
  pressed Mags or let her keep her secret at the start of the season. Pays 600 play sats once. Inspired by the old
  alert key that could message every node at once, and how it was retired by being made public.

### Season two, act three: The Old Receipt
- Ozzy now runs the Mint's watchtower, and tonight its alarm goes off: Silas the ferryman is closing his tab with
  the fishermen's co-op using a month-old receipt that says they owe him, not the other way round.
- Until the tide turns, anyone can show a newer receipt, and a cheat who is caught loses the whole tab. Help Nell
  remember which jetty her boat was on (rush her and she won't), search her boats past the deckhands' lanterns,
  and run the receipt to the harbour office. A clock on the story bar counts down to the tide.
- Miss the tide and Silas gets away with it; either way the act ends. Pays 400 play sats once. Inspired by how
  payment channels punish anyone who closes one with an old state, and why watchtowers watch all night.

### Season two, act two: The Split
- After the overflow, everyone wants an upgrade, just not the same one. Rex Bigsby, with a megaphone, wants notes
  ten times the size; Ivy says then only the rich could afford to check the maths.
- Guardians can't force rules on anyone, so the shopkeepers who take the Mint's notes decide. Win five of the six
  round Council Hill while Rex walks over to talk back anyone left alone for long. Stay with them, or split up.
- Hear Rex out or heckle him; at the count he forks off with his own coin, and a faint voice on the Mint channel
  says "Told you." Pays 350 play sats once. Inspired by the years the city argued over an upgrade.

### Season two, act one: The Overflow
- A new story tile opens season two. Rook asks Mags about the voicemail and gets "wrong number". Then Ivy calls:
  overnight, two notes worth ninety-two billion coins each were minted at the Concord Mint.
- At Mint Hall, Ivy explains how: the numbers were so big the sum ran out of room and wrapped round to something
  tiny, so the check passed. Check the notes at the Saltmarket stalls to find the three that took a bad one.
- Then a chase: Ozzy, sixteen, in a green hoodie, laps the market stalls. He sprints when you get close, turns
  back if you come at him from ahead, and runs out of puff. Catch up with him, decide what he deserves, and watch
  the guardians agree on a fix within the hour. Inspired by the night a bug minted billions of coins.
- Remembers whether you were honest at the tip in season one. Finishing pays 300 play sats once; up to four can
  play it together.

### The casebook: six side cases
- A new Casebook tile on the menu opens six one-night side cases, "ripped from the ledger": each inspired by a real
  event and retold with invented people. Play them in any order, alone or with a crew of up to four. The casebook
  marks the ones you've solved, and each pays 150 to 200 play sats the first time.
- **The Popcorn Tin.** A friendly race with Brannigan round a burglar's yard in Low Tide. He searches the obvious
  places first; the tin is in the kennel, and Biscuit the dog won't get off it unless someone rattles the bins.
- **Borrowed Number.** Pip's phone number was moved to a stranger's SIM, and his savings went with the text codes.
  Find his birthday, address and first pet out in the open, then walk into the phone shop, where the clerk isn't one.
- **Follow the Coins.** Read a public chain with Brannigan: the odd amount is the change, coins spent together share
  an owner. Find the reused address on a market tip jar (out of the music video's ring light) and the rapper who
  boasted about it.
- **The Dead Man's Vault.** An exchange boss lost at sea left his vault key in five shares on five graveyard benches;
  any three open it, which is lucky, because two plaques were stolen. Dodge Mrs Bellweather's lantern. The vault is
  empty. So was the coffin.
- **The Forgotten Password.** Kit's late brother left him a drive with three guesses left. Visit the three places
  they shared; each plays a memory of Sol that gives away a piece of his password. Then type. Both endings close
  the case.
- **Pizza Night.** Gather toppings round Saltmarket (no anchovies, ever), then run two hot pizzas across town past
  the gulls to Benny, who pays ten thousand coins for them. Ten years later, that's a lot of coins.
- Toasts now show above the story bar on phones, near the bottom of the screen, instead of hiding behind it.

### Act four: The Founder's Coins, and the signing ceremony
- The finale of season one. The little stick from pizza night holds a message signed with the Founder's key. Ellis
  Quint wants to buy it, Dr Sharpe goes on air to claim he is the Founder, and the Choir blacks out the whole city.
- Restart three of the four generators round the radio mast while Choir runners comb the dark with phone lights,
  then go live: Sharpe can't sign one sentence, and you can.
- Choose the ending as a crew: Guardian, Burn, or Byzantine, which is only offered if you once asked Teo for a cut.
  Every ending closes at Gullmoor tip at dawn, with Mags, Duncan, a mug, and a voicemail from 2009. Pays 500 once.
- Act three's "Start the signing ceremony" now opens a real ceremony room: everyone who joins is briefed, and how
  the match ends is saved and changes what Ivy says in act four.

### Act three: The Insider
- A third story tile. Brannigan has found the pattern: every family the Locksmith rang bought a wallet from the
  same shop, and its customer list leaked. Nonce, a kid hacker in the Docks, knows where the buyer logged in, if
  you ask him the right way.
- Search the dead Gate exchange's lobby terminals for the buyer's login while Gus the watchman walks the tower
  with his torch. What you find there changes everything: Teo staged his own kidnapping.
- Talk him down, hand him over, or ask what your cut would have been. Then Ivy calls: a signing ceremony, tonight.
- The end card's "Start the signing ceremony" opens a private Council room for the finale. Finishing pays 350 play
  sats once. Up to four can play it together.
- Fixed: story markers (heap signs, signed badges) could be drawn for the wrong act when a night started.

### Act two: The Ransom Call
- A new story tile on the menu. A warm voice on Teo's phone wants your twelve words by midnight. Ask for proof
  twice and listen: the ferry horn sounds at exactly the same moment both times.
- Take the words to Ivy at the Concord Mint, then get three of the five guardians round Mint Hall to sign them into
  the federation, so nobody (you included) can hand them over alone. One guardian won't sign.
- Catch the recording and you work out where Teo is yourself and beat the second lookout; miss it and Detective
  Brannigan points the way. Creep along the old breakwater behind the lookouts' torches to the lighthouse door.
- Up to four can play it together, like act one. Finishing pays 300 play sats once.
- Dawn and morning scenes are lighter than the night; the end card shows each act's own lesson.

### Play the story with friends
- Act one, The Dump, now takes a crew of up to four. The story bar shows a crew code and an "Invite friends" button
  that copies a link; friends open it (or type the code in the menu's Join box) and drop into the same night beside
  you, any time until the device is found.
- Every choice is a crew vote: you see who picked what, and can change your vote. Most votes win, a tie goes to the
  crew lead, and if time runs out the votes cast so far decide. Scenes move on once everyone has read them.
- Whoever reaches Dani or the gatehouse first starts the scene and the crew is brought to it. Everyone can dig their
  own heap; Ivor and Duncan catch players one at a time, and the crew is told who was caught.
- Everyone who finishes is paid their own first finish. Crew mates show as coloured dots on the minimap. Streamer
  mode hides the crew code on screen.

### Drive anywhere in Port Concord
- Cars are parked kerbside in every district and by the arrival point. Walk up and press E (or "Get in"); point
  where you want to go with the keys or the touch stick and the car steers there, and points behind reverse it.
  E again gets you out beside the car. Abandoned cars are towed back to their spot after four minutes.
- The server drives every car: it stops dead against walls, water, people on foot and other cars, and lets go of the
  pedal if a driver's input stops arriving. Nobody can punch, or be punched, from a car, and toughs leave drivers be.
- Other players' cars show with them inside; the camera follows yours, and the ambient traffic stops behind it.

### A living city: traffic, ships, more people, and someone to fight
- Cars drive every road, keeping left. They take turns at traffic lights, keep their distance, stop for anyone in
  the road, and fade in and out where roads meet the sea. A ferry, a cargo ship, sailboats and fishing boats sail
  the harbour and the open sea. Twice as many townsfolk walk the streets.
- Street toughs (Knuckles, Big Sal, Rivet and friends) roam the rough districts, two per district. Most only swing
  back once you start it; bullies go for anyone carrying 50 coins or more. Four punches knock one out and it drops
  a small purse, then limps off and comes back later. The server decides every punch, as for players.
- Pressing Punch on safe streets now says where brawls happen, and the rough districts are tinted red on the
  minimap. Toughs are red dots and don't count as people nearby.
- Fixed: townsfolk sank into the fronts of wide buildings, and anyone walking round a building's front corner was
  cut in half by the wall. Any guardian the camera can't see behind a building now shows as an outline.

### You see what your guardian sees
- Your guardian is never lost behind the city: buildings, trees, crates and tip heaps in front of you fade, and a
  gold outline shows you through anything still in the way.
- Buildings block sight like they would for a real person. Players and coin piles behind a building are not sent to
  you at all (so no client can peek), story characters round a corner are hidden, and the ground you cannot see is
  shaded. Someone right beside you, or half a body past a corner, still counts as seen. Market stalls don't block.

### Act one: The Dump (the first story mission)
- A new "The Dump" tile on the menu plays act one of "The Cold Key" in a private night-time Port Concord: Mr Pell's
  deadline, Dani at the hospital, Mags at the Gullmoor gatehouse, a dig past Ivor's torch and Duncan the dog, the
  crushed wallet, and Dani's paper backup. The lesson: the device was never the treasure; the twelve words were.
- Conversations type out line by line, with choices on a clock (silence is an answer), notes when someone will
  remember what you did, and clues written into the HUD. The end card lists what each character remembers.
- The server runs the whole story: choices, clocks, digs, catches. Where the wallet is buried never reaches the
  browser. The first finish pays 250 play sats once; replays save your choices only.
- Buildings in front of your guardian now fade, so you are never lost behind a tower.
- `npm run story` plays the act in headless Brave with the keyboard, on desktop and phone.

### The Mint vault (milestone 3 of the open world)
- Carry your pocket to the gold VAULT ring at the Mint Hall door and press E (or "Vault my pocket"): the coins become
  play sats in your wallet, where no punch can reach them. That is the custody lesson as gameplay.
- Up to 300 play sats a day; anything over stays in your pocket. A double-pressed deposit is paid once, and a failed
  one hands the coins back.
- The vault is marked on the minimap. `npm run city` now ends with the winner of the fight banking the winnings.
- Coins carry the ₿ mark: every pile in the street has a standing ₿ coin on top, and the pocket in the HUD shows one.
  The mark is drawn as shapes, so it looks the same on devices whose fonts lack the ₿ character. They are still
  play sats.

### Street fights in Port Concord (milestone 2 of the open world, rated Teen)
- Low Tide, the Reef Docks, Gullmoor and the Shallows are now rough streets: press F or the Punch button to swing.
  The server picks who it lands on, so a client can never hit someone out of reach or on safe streets.
- Four punches knock a guardian out with a "KO!", spinning stars and a slide whistle. They wake at home 3 seconds
  later with full health and a short shield. Health bars appear above anyone who has been hit.
- A knocked-out guardian drops their pocket as a coin pile. Quitting mid-fight drops it too. Loose coins turn up on
  rough streets, so the money is where the risk is.
- The HUD shows whether the streets are safe or rough, and the coins in your pocket. Vault money is never at risk.
- Unit and server tests cover the rules; `npm run city` now includes a fight that ends in a knockout.

## 24 November 2026

### Port Concord, the open city (milestone 1 of the open world)
- A new "Port Concord" tile on the menu opens one shared 128 × 128 tile harbour city (about 10,000 walkable tiles,
  ten times a match map). Its nine districts are the old islands reborn: the Mint, Council Hill, Signet Row,
  Saltmarket, the Reef Docks, the Heights, Low Tide, the Gullmoor tip and the Shallows islets.
- Roads with crossings, a river with bridges, a harbour with piers, a seafront boardwalk, parks, a container yard,
  the tip's rubbish mounds, and landmarks: the Mint Hall, City Hall, the dead Gate exchange tower, St Brigid's
  hospital, the fish market, the arcade, a chapel, the lighthouse and the radio mast.
- The map is generated from shared code, so server and clients agree on every tile; a test checks every walkable
  tile is reachable and every building is drawable.
- The client paints the ground in 16 × 16 tile chunks around the camera and drops far ones, one chunk per frame.
- The server sends each player only the guardians within 30 tiles (kept until 36), five times a second; emotes reach
  only those who can see you. A minimap, district arrivals and population sit in the HUD.
- `npm run city` runs a two-player browser test of all of this.

### Bigger, richer island maps
- Lightning Reef and Watchtower Peaks are grown 1.5× (48 × 42 tiles), about the Citadel's walkable size, with the
  same rooms, bridges, relays and partition rules.
- Reef: a pilastered Pearl Vault, a taller lighthouse, a canopied signing pavilion, fishers' huts, market stalls,
  parasols, drying nets, shell paths between bridges, a compass mosaic on Lagoon Square, shells and starfish.
- Peaks: alpine chalets and a herders' cabin replace the beach huts; a buttressed Ice Vault, a crenellated tower,
  cairns, woodpiles, pine stands, flagstone paths and a paved council ring on Summit Square.
- Lobby: one row of Academy, Explore and Wardrobe tiles; sound, music, graphics and streamer mode tuck behind a
  Settings button. `npm run dev:islands` serves a local copy with every island open on ports 5181 and 2568.

## 23 November 2026

### Explorer preview
- Add `?explore=all` to the client URL to open every finished island, its card and Chronicle chapters, and the Beach
  Town visit button for visual review. Preview mode does not alter rank, stars, rewards or server-side progression.
- Move the archipelago out of the crowded main menu into a full-size Map view. Its menu button and progress bar show
  how many islands remain, while the larger layout keeps island labels and the selected island card easy to scan.
- Finished islands now have an Explore action. Mint Island, Signing Isle, Council Isle, Watchtower Peaks and Lightning
  Reef open as local walkable visual previews with distinct landmarks; Beach Town keeps its full multiplayer scene.
- Each walkable island now has a local objective: prepare an ecash batch, reach a signing threshold, cast a council
  ballot, repair both relays, or open a Lightning route. Glowing markers, `E` interaction and HUD progress make the
  environments playable without changing rank, rewards or server state.

### Every island finished
- **Watchtower Peaks** has its own match map: floating mountain tops, rope bridges, pines and drifting mist. A partition
  takes down the bridges between the west and east ranges (server movement, client prediction and bot paths all follow),
  and each range holds one relay. `npm run peaks` plays it in a browser and watches a bot cut the network.
- **Academy levels for Signing Isle, Council Isle and Watchtower Peaks** (8 to 13), with four new drills: thresholds,
  the one-third rule, council evidence and network splits. Levels now open with their island. `npm run academy` reads
  each drill off the screen and answers it.
- **Seal Book tabs per island**: Signing Isle, Council Isle and Watchtower Peaks (existing seals regrouped; ids unchanged),
  with new chains and rewards: the Signet Robe, Verdict Shades and Alpine Robe, and the titles Pillar of the Mint and
  Peak Warden.
- **Codex**: new pages on threshold signatures and network partitions; the circular-economies page no longer calls Beach
  Town a later update.
- Fixed: Byzantine practice bots never sabotaged, because the readiness message that arrives as a match starts was wiped
  when the bot noticed the start.
- The lobby map picker lists all three maps; bots on the Peaks pick the relay on their own side of the cut.

### Lightning Reef
- A second match map: seven sandy islets in a lagoon, joined by plank bridges, with the Pearl Vault, a lighthouse, a
  signing pavilion, coral and a moored boat. Light pulses run along the bridges like payments through channels. The
  same rules apply everywhere through a shared arena definition (map, rooms, stations, spawns, relays, council seal).
- Hard gateway routes on the reef: the working route has 5 sats to spare at most, and the decoys fall just short.
- Private lobbies have a map picker. The reef is offered once anyone in the room has opened it (checked server-side
  from their profile); quick match stays on the Citadel. Bots follow the lobby to the reef and play there.
- Reef place names in quick chat, prompts and task panels; a Lightning Reef Seal Book tab with six seals, two titles,
  the Pearl Diver Goggles and the Reef Board; the Codex lesson on Lightning gateways explains the tighter channels.
- Tests: arena geometry for every map, server arena rules and a reef match, bots on the reef, and `npm run reef`
  (a browser walks the bridges to the Gateway Harbour).

### Smoother music
- Players can choose Adaptive music or pin any of the game themes from the new persistent Music selector; choosing Off
  silences music without muting effects.
- The music is composed bar by bar in `apps/client/src/audio/composer.ts`. Chords change by voice leading. The melody is
  a gliding legato voice in two-bar phrases that move by step and end on a chord tone. Arpeggios roll under it, played
  on electric-piano keys (a soft guitar at the beach).
- Softer instruments: swelling triangle pads, a round bass, a soft pulse instead of kicks and hi-hats, and a separate
  long, darker reverb for music. Mood changes crossfade over a few seconds.
- Fixed: the plucked-string feedback loop could ring up to infinity, because low-pass Q is in dB in Web Audio. That
  turned all sound to silence until a reload, and it affected the plucked sound effects too.
- Tests for scale, voice leading and phrasing in every mood. All ten moods and all twenty effects were recorded in
  headless Brave: no clipping, no clicks, no NaN.

: site, installable app, deploy
- The client is an installable app: a hand-written service worker with a versioned offline shell, an update banner
  that never reloads mid-match, per-path cache headers and `worker-src 'self'` (`npm run offline`).
- The Astro site in `apps/site`: landing page, nine sourced Codex lessons, share cards, sitemap, strict CSP, and
  dist and browser checks (`npm run verify -w @byzantine-council/site`).
- Production config in `deploy/` (nginx, hardened systemd unit, env template, daily database backups) and
  `docs/deploy.md`, with tests that keep the headers in sync with the code.

### Milestone 9 (started): reaching more players
- First-time tips, achievement cards, share cards, streamer mode, a graphics quality setting that defaults to Low on
  small devices, and phone layout fixes.

### Look, sound and story
- A painted world: textured stone, planks, grass and water, soft shadows, ambient occlusion and craggy cliffs.
- Physics-based effects for every action (tasks, wax seals, freezes, partitions, councils, fireworks, footsteps),
  wind-swayed trees, flocking birds, rain with splashes, thunder delayed by the speed of sound, rainbows, meteors,
  and day light that turns to dusk through a match. Each phenomenon explains itself once with a short note.
- Procedural music per place and act, plus synthesized sound effects (Karplus-Strong strings, FM bells).
- The Chronicle of Concordia: an original illustrated story in eight parts that opens with the islands.
- Guardians wear the hats, eyewear and robe trims they own; worn looks sync into matches and Beach Town.

### Security review fixes
- A finished match no longer resets the Beach Town courier daily cap (it could be farmed with solo bot matches).
  A regression test covers it.
- One network can seat at most one room's worth of bots (8) across its lobbies, so nobody can take the whole pool.
- A courier payment that fails in the store now says so and gives the parcel back for a retry. An already-spent
  job key is reported as paid, never retried. Look refreshes in town are paced to once every 10 seconds.

### Fixes
- On phones, the joystick layer no longer covers Beach Town's buttons, and dialogs sit above in-world HUDs.
  `npm run beach:phone` now runs the Beach Town visit on a 390 px touch screen.
- Academy Codex links now use the site's real slugs.
- A cookie package conflict that broke the Astro build is resolved.
